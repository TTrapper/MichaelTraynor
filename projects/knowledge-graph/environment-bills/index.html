About the data

What is here

A view of one slice of a knowledge graph of Canadian federal legislation. The graph links each bill to its printed versions, the provisions of existing Acts those versions amend, the votes taken on it, and the committee meetings that considered it. Everything on this page is read from that graph at build time.

Which bills

Two rules. Every bill in the 45th Parliament that amends one of the 22 statutes on the Statutes tab is included; the build fails if one is missing. The rest are bills on an environmental subject that amend none of those statutes, found by keyword and graph search. That second group, and the six subject headings, are editorial choices made with AI assistance.

Provisions and plain English

Each bill shows a selection of its clauses, word for word from the printed version named above them. Where a clause repeals or replaces existing law, the earlier wording is shown under it. That wording is read from the consolidation of the Act in force just before the bill became law, or from the latest one if the bill has not become law. A repealed block too long to quote is listed by heading and section title. Provisions of existing law that a clause cites by number or by definition are quoted under it as well, so the reader does not have to look them up. The graph's own links for these are mostly unresolved, so they were read off each clause the way the graph's reference resolver reads them, then looked up in the consolidation in force. The Plain English button swaps every block in a clause, the bill text and the quoted law, for a short restatement written by an AI model. Those restatements describe what the text says and nothing else. The legal text is the authority.

How to read the numbers

  • Provisions amending an Act counts distinct provisions of a bill linked to that Act, across all printed versions of the bill.
  • Royal Assent is not the same as being in force. Many Acts come into force later, by order or on a date set in the bill.
  • Votes are House of Commons votes. A member’s party is the one they sat with on the day.
  • Committee meetings in the Senate carry dates and numbers only. House meetings also carry speaking turns and witnesses.
  • Mentions are text matches on a bill number in House committee transcripts, counted from the bill’s first reading.
